# Adoption Risks and Success Signals

This draft preserves the former persona frustrations and success conditions for later use in 1-pagers. It is organized by the people whose adoption could determine whether the product works in daily restaurant service.

## Maya, Casual-Dining Guest

### Adoption risks

- The guest menu may show unavailable items or omit enough information for Maya to decide what to order.
- Maya may have to wait for a server to learn what is currently available.
- A generic QR code may be distrusted if it starts ordering, payment, account, or other unexpected flows.
- A slow or difficult phone experience may lead Maya to ignore the QR menu and return to asking the server for basic information.

### Adoption success signals

Maya scans any restaurant QR code, quickly finds an available item with the information she needs, and can tell the server what she wants without first asking for a printed menu or availability check.

## Daniel, Experienced Server

### Adoption risks

- Daniel may select the wrong table if active table labels are unclear.
- He may lose trust in the app if it permits unavailable items to be added to a new order.
- He may return to verbal kitchen checks if displayed order progress is stale.
- He may be unable to set guest expectations if rejected items lack useful explanations.
- He may revert to manual calculations if a bill has incorrect items, modifiers, quantities, or totals.

### Adoption success signals

Daniel selects the correct table, creates accurate orders from available items, sees current progress and rejection reasons, delivers items, produces an accurate bill, and closes service without paper tickets or repeated verbal status checks.

## Priya, Line Cook

### Adoption risks

- Missing or unclear modifiers can lead to incorrectly prepared items.
- Stale status information can prompt avoidable interruptions from servers.
- Priya may continue working on an item if the cancellation alert is not visible at the shared kitchen station.
- If she cannot clearly communicate that an item cannot be made, the server and guest receive information too late.

### Adoption success signals

Priya sees the items and modifiers needed for preparation, receives timely cancellation information, updates availability when the kitchen runs out of an item, and provides accurate readiness or rejection information without interrupting kitchen flow.

## Elena, Restaurant Manager

### Adoption risks

- Menu or table changes may appear in one view but not another, causing staff and guests to use conflicting information.
- Guests may see an item after the kitchen has run out of it.
- Unsupported modifiers may be selected during order entry.
- Ambiguous or reused table labels may make an existing order difficult to identify.
- Elena may abandon the product if routine menu and table maintenance requires external systems or manual workarounds.

### Adoption success signals

Elena makes a menu or table change once and sees staff and guests use the same current information. Orders remain identifiable by their associated table when that table is later deactivated.

## Marcus, New Server

### Adoption risks

- Marcus may be overwhelmed if table selection, order entry, item availability, and service progress require separate or unclear workflows.
- He may make guest-facing mistakes if he cannot quickly recognize unavailable items, kitchen updates, rejection reasons, or the final-bill process.
- He may fall back to asking coworkers for every step if the app cannot be learned during his first shift.

### Adoption success signals

Marcus can complete training and independently use the app for routine table selection, order entry, item delivery, and bill generation within one day of starting at the restaurant. He knows when to ask a more experienced server or manager for help with exceptions.
