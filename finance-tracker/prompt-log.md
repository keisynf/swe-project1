# AI Interaction Log: Personal Finance and Expense Tracker

Graded artifact, and it feeds two separate rubric lines: the detailed log of AI interactions,
and the critique of the models. Capture entries as you go. Reconstructing this afterward from
memory does not work, and the prompts that failed are the most valuable evidence in it, so
record those rather than only the wins.

Vary your prompts deliberately. "What prompts worked better" is one of the questions you owe
an answer to, and the assignment also asks for iteration and early draft versions, so this log
should show a process rather than a single clean pass per model.

## Running comparison notes

Accumulate observations here as you go so the final critique rests on evidence rather than
recollection. Dimensions worth tracking:

- Fidelity to the brief versus inventing unrequested features
- Whether the vision followed Moore's template correctly or drifted into generic marketing copy
- Honesty of the UNLIKE clause, given that the real alternatives are free and larger
- Persona quality against Chapter 3: personalization, job-related detail, education and
  technical skill, relevance, and whether it avoided the "goals" trap Sommerville argues against
- Scenario quality against Chapter 3: objective stated, persona referenced, activity detail,
  and a problem the existing tooling cannot solve
- Whether user stories stayed at story granularity or silently became epics
- Sizing rationale quality, since the rubric grades the reasoning rather than the number
- How each model responded to correction, which is often where the real difference shows

## Critique (write last)

Two things to cover, and they are not the same thing.

**The specification.** What the contrast between versions revealed about the requirements
themselves: gaps, contradictions, assumptions neither version surfaced, places where one
version was simply wrong about the product. This is what the assignment says the contrast is
for, and it is the harder and more valuable half.

**The models.** Which was better, under what circumstances, which prompts worked and which
failed, and where the weaker model still outperformed the stronger one. A split verdict with
evidence reads as a more credible finding than declaring one model the winner outright.
